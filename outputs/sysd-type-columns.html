02 / TraditionalAligned columns · Continuous reading
On design systems / An introduction

A common language,
with a personal accent.

Good foundations let different projects feel related while giving each one space to develop its own identity.

Every project begins with decisions. A typeface, a colour, the distance between a heading and the words beneath it. Individually, these choices seem small. Together, they establish how a product feels.

When those decisions are made with care and used consistently, they become a language. People learn what to expect. A familiar rhythm makes a new page easier to understand.

Start with the essentials

For sysd, the first setting is the web. Typography must work at different widths, paragraphs need room to breathe, and the hierarchy should remain clear on a small screen.

A modest set of well-chosen styles can support many situations: a quiet settings page, an expressive project story, or a useful tool. Each draws from the same foundations.

Build a recognisable voice

The aim is a professional tone that feels personal. Repeated details create recognition: the proportion of a title, the pace of a text column, the restraint of a colour palette.

As the system grows, real projects will help refine it. Keep the choices that prove useful, improve the ones that cause friction, and let the system develop through use.

03 / The space between12-column composition
Notes on attention / 01

Let the words
hold the room.

Some pages need very little. A considered title, a passage worth reading, and enough space around both to let the reader settle in. The composition draws its strength from the relationship between what is present and what is left open.

Here, the main column carries the weight of the page. Its paragraphs create a continuous texture: substantial enough to feel like an essay, narrow enough to follow comfortably. The smaller column offers a second pace, somewhere to pause without interrupting the argument.

Make space a decision

The empty columns are part of the design. They frame the text, separate the voices, and give the composition a recognisable shape. A grid makes those choices repeatable across a journal, a case study, or a personal website.

When the screen becomes smaller, the margins contract and the side note follows the main text. The reading order stays intact. The structure adapts while the relationship between the two voices remains clear.

In the margin

Leave something open.

A short observation can sit beside a longer argument. It does not need to fill the height of the page.

The space beneath it is intentional.
